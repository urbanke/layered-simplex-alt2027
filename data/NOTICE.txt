CORPUS SOURCE AND LICENSING

The corpus inputs are downloaded and prepared locally; this repository
contains their recipes and checksums, not the corpus text. Both inputs
are derived from The King James Version of the Bible, Project Gutenberg
eBook #10:
https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10

Official UTF-8 download:
https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10.txt.utf-8
Alternate official endpoint with the same pinned bytes:
https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/10/pg10.txt
Downloaded source SHA-256:
0204adaed1f25700aa854218cae63c7172228c41088f335e99167a071eed83c0

Run python scripts/get_kjv.py to prepare data/kjv.txt and
data/kjv-secondary.txt, or supply --from-file for a local copy of that
source. Source and prepared-output checksums must match before output
files are published. The accompanying manifests specify the recipes,
counts and exact prepared text hashes:

Canonical: 915,860 tokens, 13,550 distinct types.
SHA-256: 27c79dc3ad00db78e55a09f9d0140470e42ce11bd41099a2972712fbe2e48196
Secondary: 917,868 tokens, 13,554 distinct types.
SHA-256: b6f472f38d88a2efcfc000295560a92483da3e7b0ade00fe3f30160cf27f9560

The secondary recipe includes explicit presentation normalization to
reproduce the fixed control: omit the initial testament heading, convert
the one double hyphen to an em dash, and place the three-asterisk separator
at the end before word-and-punctuation tokenization. These conventions
are recorded in the preprocessing script and secondary manifest.
